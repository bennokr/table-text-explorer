# Folding rules for English: inflected forms of one word get the same key, so book/books, make/making and
# party/parties count as one when near-duplicate phrases are merged and when cluster names avoid repeating a word.
# The key is only ever used for that comparison. It never changes what is shown, counted or queried, and it is
# deliberately light: it will sometimes merge two words and miss irregular forms (go/went).
#
# Rules apply once, top to bottom: the first whose suffix matches, leaving at least `minimum` letters, wins.
#   suffix  replacement  minimum      (a replacement of - means nothing; a rule that maps a suffix to itself
#                                      protects words such as "class" and "status" from the plural rule)
# Then the directives run: undouble (a doubled last consonant becomes one: runn -> run) and drop-e (a last e goes:
# make -> mak, so make, makes and making agree).
